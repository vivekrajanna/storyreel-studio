### VO
Starlight travels across trillions of miles of calm, empty space in a perfectly straight line.
But the very last second before reaching your eyes, that light plunges into Earth's atmosphere.
Our sky is like a giant swirling ocean of warm and cool air pockets!
Every time starlight hits a moving pocket of air, it bends slightly left and right, making the star look like it is twinkling!

### Rules
- 🪐 | 1. Spot a Planet! | Planets look closer and bigger than stars, so they shine steadily without twinkling.
- 🏔️ | 2. Look Straight Up! | Stars near the horizon twinkle more because their light passes through thicker air.
- 🛰️ | 3. Space Telescopes! | Telescopes in space sit above Earth's air, so stars stay razor-sharp!

### VO
Next time you step outside at night, try these three super-easy stargazing tricks!
First, spot a steady, non-twinkling bright dot — you are probably looking at a planet like Jupiter or Venus!
Second, notice how stars low near the horizon twinkle the wildest because their light crosses more air.
And third, remember why space telescopes orbit above the sky — no air means crystal-clear stars!
